Example setup

Four strings, keyboard and horn

The keyboard’s A4 fixes String I’s second partial at 440 Hz. From there, four successive pure fifths tune Strings II, III, IV and the horn. The red cords show which partials meet.

Open the keyboard’s column menu and change its A4 from 440 to 442 Hz. String I moves from 220 to 221 Hz, and every instrument in the chain follows. The 31-EDO lane gives you an independent reference.

Play and compare

Click a horizontal segment in the Pitch map or a cell in either table. The inspector shows its sounding frequency, deviations from your tuning references, and the closest pitches on the other instruments. Click a match to hear both tones together for six seconds. A positive value means the other instrument sounds higher.

Find your way around

The pitch map and tables each use nearly a full screen of height. Choose Collapse in the green spectrum overlay to bring the table closer to the top of the page. On narrow screens, Inspector and Back to pitches jump between panels.

  • Pitch map spaces pitches by cents or Hz. Choose Closest pair to magnify a small interval or Fit all to see the whole range.
  • Once zoomed in, use the vertical slider to travel across the range. Its end buttons move by most of a screen. You can also scroll or drag to pan, or pinch or Ctrl/⌘-scroll to zoom.
  • By partial or By index lines up the same row number across sources. By pitch groups pitches by the nearest 12-EDO note.

Sources and tuning

Choose Add source. A harmonic source takes a sounding fundamental in Hz. For example, a 220 Hz string produces partials at 220, 440 and 660 Hz. A tempered instrument takes an octave division count or a list of ascending degrees in cents, starting at 0. Give each instrument its own reference frequency and offset.

For an 88-key, 12-EDO piano, choose −48 as the first displayed step and 88 pitches in the toolbar. Step 0 is that instrument’s A4. Use the column menu to change its tuning, name, group or colour, or to move it left or right.

Tuning sets concert A4 and the reference systems used for comparison. To retune an instrument, edit its source. Tuning lanes draws the active references beside the instrument columns.

Link pitches

Select the pitch that will drive the tuning. In the inspector choose Link pitch (or Link partial), then choose a destination source and pitch. The preview shows the destination’s new tuning. Choose Create link to make both endpoints sound at the same frequency.

The solid knot marks the driving pitch and the ring marks the follower. Retuning the driver propagates through the chain. Select a link in the inspector to bring it into view. Unlink releases the follower at its current tuning.

MIDI and sharing

To hear your sample libraries, choose MIDI I/O → Connect MIDI and select a hardware or virtual output. In your DAW, receive that port on the channel assigned to each source. Match the pitch-bend range on both ends (the default is ±2 semitones). Sound off mutes the browser sine tones while MIDI playback continues.

To play a spectrum from a keyboard or DAW, select an Input port. For the cello, set In channel to 1 and First key to 60 (C4). Play 60 for the fundamental (P01), 61 for the octave (P02) and 62 for P03. Each higher key plays the next displayed partial. On a tempered source the same keys play successive scale steps.

Hold a key to sustain its pitch and release it to stop. Each source sounds its most recent key, so you can play several sources together on different channels. Follow out makes a source listen on its output channel. To use a sample library, send controller notes into PolySpectra and receive its output on a separate DAW port. The outgoing note and pitch bend give the partial’s sounding pitch and the note carries your original velocity.

Share setup → Copy link saves your instruments, partial links, tuning references, MIDI channels, first keys, selection and view in the URL. An example or shared setup opens separately from the workspace saved on your device.
